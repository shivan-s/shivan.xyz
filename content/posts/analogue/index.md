When I started reading _Digital Minimalism_ by [Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/), it's made me think about using technology to best serve me. The problem with technology such as smartphones is that they are both very useful and very distracting - all combined in one device.

So, there comes a point where I want to take some notes on my phone, but then I end up scrolling the web, wasting time and attention that could be used elsewhere.

Acknowleding this, I have purchased items inadvertenly appealing to an analogue lifestyle.

Let me go through those items...

## Fountain Pen

<Figure src={FountainPen} alt="A fountain pen illuminated by natural light; in the background is lined paper with handwritten text saying, 'I got a new fountain pen'" caption="My new fountain pen" />

The idea is that I want to journal more (in a handwritten journal), so maybe a better pen would help? A fountain pen is more expensive than regular pens - so I'm much less inclined to lose it. Also, regular pens, when they are all used up, get tossed away. With a fountain pen, you would fill it up with ink, so there is less waste.

<Figure src={Ink} alt="Bottle of ink called 'Jacques Herbin', subtitle 'Paris - 1670', and with a style 'Noir inspiration'" caption="Ink for my fountain pen" />

I got lovely scented ink too so that makes the process of writing even better. On top of that, fountain pens often involve almost no pressure when writing, improving the experience even further.

## Analogue Watch

The main purpose of a watch is to tell the time (any maybe day of the week and date for the month). However, now we have smart watches, which also tell the time but do more like monitor health biometrics, GPS, provide notifications and the list goes on.

These are too many features that provide minimal benefit, but a quite drastic detriments, which is constant connection. Newport in **Digital Minimalism** talks about two things: 1. solitude deprivation, where we don't disconnect complete from outside stimuli (e.g. constant connections and notifications); 2. marginal benefits, where smart watches do provide some benefits, like a computer on your wrist, but these benefits are outweighted by the first point and being without these small benefits maybe a minor nuisance but overall are better for you.

Cue the analogue watch.
